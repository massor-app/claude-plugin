# Programming principles

Defaults for drafting. The trainer's preferences always win; these fill the gaps.

## Splits

- Up to three days a week, especially for newer clients: full-body sessions.
- Four days: upper/lower is the usual default.
- Five or more: push/pull/legs or body-part focus, when recovery and experience support it.
- Every week in a program has exactly the same number of training days.

## Movement coverage

Across the week, cover:

- Horizontal push and horizontal pull
- Vertical push and vertical pull
- Knee dominant (squat, lunge, split squat)
- Hip dominant (hinge, bridge, thrust)
- Core and anti-movement (planks, carries, dead bugs, Pallof presses)

Pair pushes with pulls and knee with hip work to keep sessions balanced. When the equipment rules a pattern out (say, no vertical pull with dumbbells only), name the gap to the trainer and offer the closest substitute.

## Exercise selection

- Keep selection simple and repeatable. Variety for its own sake slows progress.
- Put priority work and higher-skill compound lifts first in a session.
- Avoid two near-identical movements in one session unless the goal calls for deliberate extra volume.
- Beginners do best on stable, lower-skill options: dumbbells, machines, cables, bodyweight.
- Prefer exercises the client already trains (see `get_exercise_history`) when they fit, so starting loads are known.

## Volume, effort, load

- Typical working effort is RPE 7–8. Earlier weeks and newer lifters sit lower.
- Avoid absolute loads unless there's a tested or recently logged number to base them on. Otherwise guide load with reps and effort.
- Match session volume to the session length. Supersets and circuits save time for accessories; keep demanding compound lifts as straight sets.
- Keep hard conditioning secondary to strength work and suited to the client's recovery.

## Progressive programs (4–8 weeks)

- Make progression explicit week to week: reps, sets, effort, load guidance, or exercise variation.
- A sensible default arc: a baseline week, gradual overload, a harder stimulus, and a lower-fatigue final week. Stretch or compress it to fit the duration.
- Write out every week in full.

## Reusable programs (one week, repeated)

- Write one representative week.
- Put the repeat-and-progress rule in the program summary, e.g. "When every set hits the top of the rep range at RPE 8 or below, go up one dumbbell size."
